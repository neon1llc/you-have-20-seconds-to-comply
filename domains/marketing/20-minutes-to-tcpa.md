**Read this if** your business sends text messages to customers (appointment reminders, order updates, promotions, or two-factor codes), makes sales calls, or uses software that dials or texts automatically. It's one of the most litigated consumer laws in the country, so it matters even for small businesses.

## What the TCPA is

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) limits calls and text messages to consumers. Courts treat a text message as a call under the law. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) writes the rules. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule adds requirements for telemarketing and runs the National Do Not Call Registry.

What makes the TCPA expensive is that consumers can sue directly. Statutory damages are **$500 per call or text**, and up to **$1,500** if the violation was willful or knowing. A campaign of a few thousand messages to people who didn't consent can turn into a class action worth millions.

## Consent: the core rule

The consent you need depends on the technology and the purpose.

| Message | Consent needed |
|---|---|
| Informational texts or autodialed or prerecorded calls to a cell phone (appointment reminders, order status, account alerts) | Prior express consent. Giving you the number for that purpose generally counts. |
| Marketing texts or autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing calls to a cell phone | Prior express written consent: a signed agreement (an electronic signature, such as a checkbox on a web form, can work) that clearly authorizes marketing messages to that number, and says consent isn't a condition of buying anything. |
| Prerecorded or artificial voice telemarketing calls to a residential landline | Prior express written consent. |
| Live, manually dialed sales calls | No TCPA consent needed, but Do Not Call rules and calling hours apply. |
| Emergency messages | Exempt. |

In 2021 the Supreme Court narrowed the definition of an "automatic telephone dialing system" in *Facebook v. Duguid*, which reduced some autodialer claims. Many states have since passed their own laws with broader definitions, and prerecorded and artificial voice rules still apply, including to AI-generated voices. Plan as if consent rules apply to every automated text.

The FCC adopted a rule in 2023 that would have required consent to be given to one seller at a time ("one-to-one consent"). A federal appeals court struck it down in January 2025, so it isn't in effect. Consent still has to be clear, specific to your business, and provable.

## Do Not Call and calling hours

- **National Do Not Call Registry.** Before telemarketing calls or texts, check numbers against the registry at least every 31 days. There are exceptions for existing business relationships (within limits) and for people who gave written permission.
- **Internal do-not-call list.** Keep your own list, honor requests to stop, and have a written policy for it. Train the people who make calls.
- **Calling hours.** No telemarketing calls or texts before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time. Some states set narrower hours.
- **Caller ID.** Telemarketers must transmit caller ID with a number the consumer can call back to opt out.

## Opt-outs

Since April 11, 2025, FCC rules say a consumer can revoke consent **in any reasonable way**, including replying with words like stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel, or unsubscribe, or other words that clearly mean stop. Businesses must honor the request **within a reasonable time, and no later than 10 business days**. You may send one confirmation text within five minutes, without marketing content, which can ask which message types to stop if the consumer gets more than one kind.

The FCC delayed, then revised in 2026, the rules on whether one opt-out stops every type of message from a business. Expect that a "stop" to marketing ends all marketing, and check the current rules before relying on narrower treatment.

## Carrier rules for business texting

Wireless carriers add requirements of their own for application-to-person texting. Businesses texting from ordinary 10-digit numbers must register their brand and campaigns (known as 10DLC registration), and toll-free numbers need verification. Carriers follow CTIA's messaging principles: clear opt-in, the business's name in messages, opt-out instructions, and HELP and STOP keywords that work. Unregistered or noncompliant traffic gets filtered or blocked.

## State laws

Several states, including Florida and Oklahoma, have their own telemarketing laws with private lawsuits, stricter consent definitions, and narrower calling hours. Check the states where your customers live.

## What staff need to know

1. **Text or call only numbers with the right consent** for that kind of message, recorded in the system.
2. **Marketing needs written consent.** A customer giving their number for an appointment reminder hasn't agreed to promotions.
3. **Honor every opt-out**, by text, phone, email, or in person, and log it the same day. The deadline is 10 business days, but sooner is safer.
4. **Respect quiet hours** in the recipient's time zone.
5. **Check Do Not Call lists** before sales calls and campaigns.
6. **Don't text from personal phones** to customers for business. Use the approved system so consent and opt-outs are tracked.
7. **Identify the business** in every message.
8. **Ask before using AI voices** or prerecorded messages. They need consent.

## What the business needs on paper

- Consent records for each number: when, how, the exact language shown, and what the person agreed to.
- The opt-in language on forms, and the keywords and replies your system uses.
- A written do-not-call policy and the internal do-not-call list.
- Proof of National Do Not Call Registry scrubs, if you telemarket.
- 10DLC or toll-free verification records.
- Training records. See the [training log](../../templates/training-log.csv).
- Contracts with texting vendors and lead sellers, with consent and compliance terms.

## Penalties and enforcement

Consumers can sue for $500 per violation, up to $1,500 if willful or knowing, and class actions are common. State attorneys general and the FCC also enforce the law, and the FTC enforces the Telemarketing Sales Rule. Carriers can block your numbers.

## Common mistakes

- Sending promotions to numbers collected for reminders or receipts.
- Buying leads without proof of consent naming your business.
- Opt-outs stored in one tool and ignored by another.
- Texting at 7 a.m. to customers in another time zone.
- Prechecked consent boxes, or consent bundled into terms of service.
- No record of what the opt-in form said when the person signed up.
