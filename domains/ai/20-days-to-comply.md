Read [20 minutes to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework](20-minutes-to-nist-ai-rmf.md) and, if you hire in New York City, [20 minutes to NYC Local Law 144](20-minutes-to-nyc-local-law-144.md) first. Each day below is one to two hours of work for the person leading the effort. The 20 days are a learning pace, not a deadline. Finishing the plan doesn't make a business compliant, and this plan is training, not legal advice or a compliance determination. If a step is already done, check it and move on. The order matters more than the day numbers: inventory before risk, and risk before policy.

## The plan

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Name the person responsible for AI use. |
| 2 | Ask staff which AI tools they use for work, including free ones and browser extensions. Make it safe to answer honestly. |
| 3 | List AI features inside software you already pay for, such as email, office suites, CRM, and meeting tools. Note which are switched on. |
| 4 | For each tool, note what data goes into it and what the vendor's terms say about training and retention. |
| 5 | Pick approved tools. Prefer business accounts with data protection terms. |
| 6 | Write the data rules: what can never go into an AI tool, and what can. |
| 7 | Write the human review rule: what AI output a person must check before it goes out or is relied on. |
| 8 | List high-impact uses: hiring, lending, pricing, medical, legal, or anything that decides something about a person. |
| 9 | For each high-impact use, check the laws that apply, including NYC Local Law 144 for hiring tools. |
| 10 | For NYC hiring tools: confirm the bias audit date, the posted summary, and the candidate notices. |
| 11 | Write the AI use policy, one or two pages, using the parts above. |
| 12 | Turn off AI features that send regulated data to vendors without approval. |
| 13 | Check marketing and product claims about AI for accuracy. |
| 14 | Decide when to tell customers AI is involved, such as chatbots and AI-generated images. |
| 15 | Schedule AI literacy training. Use the [facilitator guide](../../training/facilitator-guide.md). |
| 16 | Run the training. Collect policy [acknowledgments](../../templates/acknowledgment-form.md) and record it in the [training log](../../templates/training-log.csv). |
| 17 | Try a few prompt injection and data leak checks on any AI agent or chatbot you run. |
| 18 | Set up a way to report AI problems, and add AI incidents to the incident response plan. |
| 19 | Review one high-impact use end to end with the NIST AI RMF functions: govern, map, measure, manage. |
| 20 | Set the calendar: quarterly tool inventory updates, annual training, policy review, and audits of high-impact uses. |

## After day 20

- Every quarter: update the AI tool inventory, since vendors add AI features often.
- Every year: AI literacy training, policy review, and a check of high-impact uses. For NYC hiring tools, a new bias audit before the old one turns a year old.
- Before adopting any new AI tool: check data terms and add it to the approved list.
- When something goes wrong: treat a leak or harmful output as an incident.
