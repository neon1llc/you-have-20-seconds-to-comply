Read [20 minutes to FERPA](20-minutes-to-ferpa.md) and, if you build or choose tools for children under 13, [20 minutes to COPPA](20-minutes-to-coppa.md) first. Each day below is one to two hours of work for the person leading the effort. The 20 days are a learning pace, not a deadline. Finishing the plan doesn't make a business compliant, and this plan is training, not legal advice or a compliance determination. If a step is already done, check it and move on. The order matters more than the day numbers: inventory before risk, and risk before policy.

## The plan

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Name the person responsible for student privacy. In New York, that's the data protection officer. |
| 2 | Find your annual FERPA notice and directory information notice. Check that they're current and that they define school official and legitimate educational interest. |
| 3 | Pull the directory information opt-out list and make sure the offices that release information can see it. |
| 4 | List every system that holds student records: the student information system, learning platforms, email, file shares, and paper files. |
| 5 | List every app and online service used with students, including ones teachers adopted on their own. |
| 6 | For each app, find the contract or terms. Mark which ones meet the school official conditions and which don't. |
| 7 | Stop using, or start contracts for, apps with no acceptable terms. Check COPPA for any tool used with children under 13. |
| 8 | Edtech vendors: decide whether your service is child-directed, mixed audience, or general audience, and write down why. |
| 9 | Edtech vendors: write or update the COPPA written information security program and data retention policy, and publish the retention policy in your privacy notice. |
| 10 | Review who can see which records in each system. Limit access to staff with a legitimate educational interest. |
| 11 | Turn on multi-factor authentication for staff accounts in the student information system and email. |
| 12 | Write down how records requests are received and tracked so the 45-day deadline is met. |
| 13 | Check the record of disclosures process for releases outside the school. |
| 14 | In New York: confirm the Parents' Bill of Rights is posted and attached to vendor contracts, and collect each vendor's data privacy and security plan. |
| 15 | Write or update the incident response procedure for student data, including state reporting. |
| 16 | Schedule annual data privacy and security training. Use the [facilitator guide](../../training/facilitator-guide.md). |
| 17 | Run the training. Collect [acknowledgments](../../templates/acknowledgment-form.md) and record it in the [training log](../../templates/training-log.csv). |
| 18 | Give teachers a short list of approved apps and how to request a new one. |
| 19 | Test one parent records request from start to finish. Time it. |
| 20 | Set the calendar: annual notices, annual training, vendor contract reviews, access reviews, and the yearly app inventory. |

## After day 20

- At the start of each school year: the FERPA annual notice, the directory information notice and opt-out window, and the approved app list.
- Every year: data privacy and security training (required in New York), a vendor contract review, and an access review.
- Before any new app is used with students: a contract or data privacy agreement, and a COPPA check for children under 13.
- On request: records inspection within 45 days.
