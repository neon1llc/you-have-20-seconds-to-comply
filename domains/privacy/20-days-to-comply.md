Read [20 minutes to state privacy laws](20-minutes-to-state-privacy-laws.md) first. Each day below is one to two hours of work for the person leading the effort. If a step is already done, check it and move on. The order matters more than the day numbers: inventory before risk, and risk before policy.

## The plan

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Name the person responsible for privacy and security. |
| 2 | List the states where your customers and employees live. Note which have comprehensive privacy laws and whether you meet their thresholds. |
| 3 | Inventory personal data: what you collect, where it's stored, who can see it, and why you keep it. |
| 4 | Mark the data that counts as private information under breach laws: Social Security, license, and account numbers, biometrics, health data, and login credentials. |
| 5 | Delete personal data you don't need, starting with old exports, spreadsheets, and former customers' files. |
| 6 | Turn on multi-factor authentication for email, file storage, and any system holding personal data. |
| 7 | Confirm encryption on laptops, phones, and backups. |
| 8 | List vendors that receive personal data. Check that contracts limit their use and require security. |
| 9 | Review the website for ad and analytics tags. Decide whether they amount to selling or sharing data under the laws that apply, and add an opt-out and Global Privacy Control support if needed. |
| 10 | Compare the privacy notice to what you actually do. Fix the differences. |
| 11 | If a comprehensive state law applies, set up a way to receive, verify, track, and answer privacy requests on time. |
| 12 | Write a short security program using the SHIELD Act's list as the outline. |
| 13 | Write a retention schedule for each kind of personal data. |
| 14 | Write an incident response plan with the New York 30-day deadline and the state agencies to notify. |
| 15 | Schedule privacy and security training. Use the [facilitator guide](../../training/facilitator-guide.md). |
| 16 | Run the training. Collect [acknowledgments](../../templates/acknowledgment-form.md) and record it in the [training log](../../templates/training-log.csv). |
| 17 | Run a 30-minute tabletop exercise: an employee's email account is taken over. Who does what, and who gets notified? |
| 18 | Do a data protection assessment for any higher-risk processing a state law covers, such as targeted advertising or sensitive data. |
| 19 | Test one privacy request from start to finish. |
| 20 | Set the calendar: annual training, annual review of the notice and data inventory, vendor reviews, and scheduled data disposal. |

## After day 20

- Every year: training, a review of the privacy notice against actual practice, a refresh of the data inventory, and vendor reviews.
- On a schedule: disposal of data past its retention period.
- When anything changes: a new state with customers, a new vendor, or a new website tool means a fresh look at which laws apply.
- After a breach: notices within the strictest applicable deadline, including New York's 30 days.
